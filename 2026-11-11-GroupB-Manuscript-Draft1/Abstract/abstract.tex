\begin{abstract}
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{\baselineskip}

Smallholder and urban growers in the Philippines manage irrigation largely by
inspection, and the decision of when and how much to water is made without a
quantitative record of how the crop is responding. This study developed and
deployed an automated system that monitors the growth of pechay
(\textit{Brassica rapa} subsp. \textit{chinensis}) under three controlled soil
moisture regimes and relates the observed canopy development to the harvested
biomass.

A nine-pot array was instrumented inside a purpose-built greenhouse enclosure
on the University of San Carlos Talamban Campus. Each pot carries a capacitive
soil moisture probe and an individually valved drip line driven by an ESP32-S3
controller on a custom board, which maintains three treatment bands of 30 to
40, 50 to 60 and 70 to 80 per cent volumetric water content by a
dose-and-wait rule. A Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W with a Camera Module 3 captures a
fixed overhead frame at local noon each day, from which a Green Pixel Ratio is
computed for each pot by thresholding in the HSV colour space over a
non-overlapping per-pot measurement area. The segmentation was validated
against independent manual annotation by two labellers.
